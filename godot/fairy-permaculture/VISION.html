Fairy Permaculture The Soil Engine · Progression Vision · April 2026

The player arrives at a parcel of depleted coastal BC land. Trees stand, rocks rest, streams run, ruins hint at past failure. The soil is grey-brown, lifeless. The job: heal it into living forest. The currency is not coins or fairy food — it is soil. Every system of the farm funnels into building it.

This document shows the soil engine in full, the production routes for every amendment, how the player automates the work with fairies and animals, how plant-animal synergies compound, and how progression rings layer easy-to-manage over hard-to-master depth.

Design pillarsSix locked principles

Soil is the currency. Every system funnels into building it. Compost, animals, plants, earthworks — all soil-building loops.
No waste. Manure, urine, bones, prunings, runoff — every by-product feeds another loop. No trash bucket.
Paired feedback. Every action + every state change = visual + audio confirmation. No silent successes.
No dead time. Slow-cooking systems ship with a parallel action. Player always has something world-building to do.
Everything is material. Trees, rocks, ruins, streams — raw stuff, not set-dressing. Click anything, work with it.
Easy to manage, hard to master. Defaults run themselves. Depth (rotation, cover-crop sequencing, mycoremediation, biochar) is discoverable.

Water & moisture retentionThe other half of soil

Dry soil grows nothing. Soaked soil rots. Moisture is the limiting factor through BC's dry summer — and the mechanic that rewards the player's earthworks, mulch, shade, and OM discipline. Retention is not one stat: it's a stack of modifiers that compound. A climax tile can hold 5× the water of a barren tile from the same rain.

Per-tile water state

Moisture

0–100 % — the current fraction of field capacity held in the root zone.

Field capacity

The max this tile can hold before drainage. A function of texture, OM, mulch, structure.

Wilting point

Moisture floor below which plants stop drawing water. Higher in clay, lower with biology.

Infiltration rate

How fast rain soaks in vs. runs off. Gated by compaction, aggregates, living roots.

Evap rate

Daily surface loss. Halved by mulch, halved again by canopy shade, cut 30 % by windbreaks.

Slope & aspect

South-facing + steep = dries fast. North-facing + shaded = holds moisture. Earthworks flatten this.

Daily water balance

Gains

  • Rain — climate-driven; BC wet winter, dry summer
  • Stream / pond sub-irrigation — tiles within 6 m of water auto-wick
  • Swale infiltration — redirects slope runoff into the berm
  • Keyline plow — moves water from wet valleys to dry ridges
  • Dew condensation — cool nights + thick mulch trap atmospheric moisture
  • Irrigation — manual (watering can), fairy route (buckets), piped from pond

Losses

  • Surface evaporation — 2–8 %/day in summer sun; mulch halves it, shade halves it again
  • Plant transpiration — heavy crops pull 5–10 %/day during fruiting
  • Runoff — 20–80 % of rain lost on bare slopes; ~0 % on swaled contour
  • Deep drainage — water past root zone on sandy / compacted / over-saturated tiles
  • Wind — +30–50 % evap on exposed tiles; windbreaks the answer

The retention flywheel

More OM → more water held → happier mycorrhizae → more glomalin glues soil into aggregates → more porosity → rain infiltrates instead of running off → roots go deeper → plants thrive → more biomass → more OM.

Break any link in this chain — bare soil, compaction, tillage, overgrazing — and the flywheel reverses. Drought exposes whoever's been skipping the work.

Drought mechanic (BC summer event)

10+ rain-free days triggers a drought banner. Tile moisture drops daily. Tiles falling under wilting point show drooping plants, halted growth, and — if prolonged — plant death. The player's tools, in order of leverage:

  • Already-built earthworks — swaled orchard tiles barely notice a 2-week drought. This is the reward for prior work.
  • Mulch — fairies can chop-and-drop comfrey/grass onto stressed tiles (fast, cheap)
  • Shade — a tall tree's drip-zone holds moisture for ~8 m around; nurse trees matter
  • Irrigation — healer fairies with watering cans; later, gravity-fed pond lines
  • Pond draw-down — long droughts eat pond reserves; large ponds buffer entire orchards
  • Drought-tolerant plantings — south-facing ridges should be herbs + grape, not lettuce

Primary macrosNPK production routes

Each macro has its own production paths. Every route is the permaculture-accurate by-product of some other loop on the farm. The player collects by-products from animals, plants, and the landscape, then crafts or applies them.

N — Nitrogen

Greens · leafy growth · quick
  • Legume cover crops clover, vetch, fava — Rhizobium symbiosis, 50–250 kg N/ha/yr
  • Chicken manure hottest + fastest of the manures
  • Cow / horse / goat manure cooler, slower release, volume
  • Urine (dilute 10:1) concentrated soluble N — catch it
  • Fish emulsion (FAA) fish trim + brown sugar, 7-day ferment, KNF
  • Blood meal culling by-product, fastest N burn
  • Nettle tea fermented stinging nettle, 2-wk brew
  • Compost tea (aerated) 24–36 h brew, apply within 4 h
  • Coffee grounds mild, acidifying — blueberries love it

P — Phosphorus

Roots · flowers · fruits · slow
  • Bone meal crushed + heated culled-animal bones, 15–30 % P
  • WCP (water-soluble Ca-P) roasted bones + vinegar, KNF
  • Fish bones / fish meal from fish trim, same profile as bone meal
  • Rock phosphate quarried from outcrops, long release
  • Bat guano from cave POI, premium
  • Seabird guano coastal-only, very concentrated
  • Chicken manure moderate P alongside N
  • Compost slow P mineralization baseline

K — Potassium

Stress resistance · fruit quality · color
  • Comfrey tea THE permaculture K source, Bocking 14 × 3 cuts/yr
  • Wood ash from hearth / biochar kiln, 5–10 % K, raises pH
  • Kelp / seaweed coastal forage, K + full micros
  • Banana-peel tea kitchen-scrap soak, 3-day brew
  • Alfalfa meal K + N, also dynamic accumulator
  • Greensand / granite dust ancient ocean sediment, long release
  • Hardwood ash higher K than softwood

Every row is collected from or crafted from a by-product already in the player's farm. Nothing is abstract fertilizer-in-a-bag.

Life below the soilBiology is half the engine

Where the magic livesPlant-animal synergies

A mature farm isn't 10 separate systems running in parallel. It's one system where every piece makes the others better. These are the famous stacks every permaculture book preaches.

Cow → Chicken (5-day lag) — Salatin mob-then-sanitize

Cows graze a paddock high-density for a day, drop manure. Five days later the fly larvae in the manure are peak nutrition and the chickens move in, scratching the pats apart and eating the larvae. The manure spreads evenly, gets incorporated, and soil OM jumps ~300 % vs. either species alone.

Pig → Chicken → Fruit tree

Pigs rootle-clear a patch of forest (removing saplings + disturbing soil). Chickens follow to sanitize + spread seed. A fruit tree planted into the resulting bed hits the ground running. Mark Shepard's "Restoration Agriculture" loop.

Duck + Rice paddy (aigamo)

Ducks patrol the paddy, eating slugs and weed seeds. Their manure feeds the rice. Azolla (N-fixing fern) floats alongside. No pesticides, no synthetic fert — Furuno's system in Japan.

Three Sisters — Haudenosaunee classic

Corn is a vertical trellis + heavy N-feeder. Bean climbs corn + fixes N for corn. Squash covers the ground with big prickly leaves — shades out weeds, deters raccoons. Together they yield ~30 % more calories per area than corn alone.

Apple guild — Hemenway classic

Comfrey at the drip line mines K from deep soil and chops-and-drops it onto the tree's feeder roots. Yarrow flowers attract hoverflies that eat aphids. Clover matrix fixes N in the grass. Alliums deter apple maggot. Daffodils at the edge scare off voles. All one guild.

Chop-and-drop + mycorrhizae

The comfrey you cut drops K-rich leaves onto the tile. Underground, the fungal network ties the fruit tree's roots to neighboring trees, sharing phosphorus in exchange for sugar. The tree pays fungi 10–30 % of its photosynthate for water + P + defense. You water once; the fungi do the rest.

Cover crop → mob graze → chop-and-drop (the triple stack)

Sow crimson clover + winter rye + tillage radish in fall. In spring, mob graze cattle for a day to press seed into soil. Immediately chop-and-drop what they miss. Soil builds 1–2 % OM in a single season — real farms measure this.

Working with the shape of the landEarthworks the player builds

A farm's water, wind, and thermal patterns are set by topography and built infrastructure. Every earthwork the player places tunes the microclimate — more soil, less erosion, longer growing season, better microbiome.

Swale

On-contour ditch + berm. Slows rainwater, infiltrates groundwater, sediment collects on the berm → trees planted there get constant water + accumulated OM.

Keyline plow

Yeomans-style sub-soiling off-contour. Moves water from wet valleys to dry ridges. Rapid topsoil building when paired with mob grazing.

Hugelkultur bed

Buried woody core under soil + compost. Wood rots into a 10-year OM reservoir + water sponge. Halves irrigation demand.

Pond

Water storage, frost buffer, fire break, duck habitat, aquaculture, reflected light for adjacent orchards. High-leverage structure.

Windbreak

Multi-row tree hedge at 40–60 % permeability. Reduces evaporation ~30 %, protects tender crops, wildlife corridor, bonus fruit + nuts.

Check dam

Small dams (rock, woven willow, log) stop gully erosion. Sediment builds behind them — former gullies become fertile swales.

Terrace

Flat growing on steep slopes. Captures runoff; each terrace holds its own microclimate and moisture profile.

Rock garden / thermal mass

Stones absorb day heat, radiate at night. Extends season 2–4 weeks. Mediterranean herbs thrive against south-facing stone walls.

No wasteClosing the loops

A mature permaculture farm is a closed circle. Every output loops back. The game makes these loops visible as UI threads — when you capture a by-product, the game tells you what it feeds.

Chicken eats kitchen scraps → lays egg (fairy food / Ca source) + drops manure → compost pile heats 15 % faster → compost applied to orchard tile → apple ripens larger + faster.
Comfrey grows on a tile → chopped by fairy → drops as K-rich mulch at drip line of fruit tree → tree K-up, produces more fruit → fruit feeds fairies → more fairies do more chopping.
Culled animal bones → roasted in kiln → soaked in vinegar → WCP (water-soluble P) → drenched at flowering fruit tree → bigger crop → more fruit scraps → back to chickens.
Fairy / worker urine → diluted 10:1 in water → applied to cover crop patch → explosive N-loving growth → chop-and-drop → feeds soil and next season's vegetables.
Wood from felled tree → half to building shed, half to biochar kiln → biochar + compost = charged biochar → applied to tile → CEC bumps permanently → soil holds nutrients for decades.
Roof runoff → rain barrel → filtered through reed bed → irrigation + greywater returns reed biomass to compost → soil.

Soil is not a bar that fills up. It's a living matrix of NPK, micros, pH, biology, water — shaped by every plant you grow, every animal you pasture, every amendment you craft from your own waste stream.

Building it is the game. Watching it come alive is the reward.

Interactions & food web Who helps who · who eats who · who blocks whom

Permaculture is relational. Every species has a web of friends, enemies, and neighbors that change its effective yield. This tab shows the major food-web tiers, companion/antagonist matrices, and the closed loops the player discovers over the course of a run.

Food web tiersWhat feeds what on a healthy BC farm

Sun & mineralsenergy & substrate
Sunlight → plants via photosynthesis. Weathered rock → minerals via fungal + root acids.
Producersprimary — build biomass
All plants. Pioneers & cover crops on poor soil; orchard & canopy on rich soil. They convert sun + CO₂ + water into sugars + leaves + fruit.
Decomposersreturn & recycle
Bacteria, archaea, fungi (saprophytic), earthworms, BSF larvae, isopods, springtails, millipedes. Turn dead plant matter back into soil-accessible nutrients.
Mycorrhizaeunderground trade network
Fungal partnership with plant roots. Plants pay sugar; fungi deliver water + P + trade between plants. Gates access to higher-tier plants.
Primary consumersplant eaters
Pollinators (bees, hoverflies, butterflies). Grazers (goat, sheep, cow, rabbit). Gleaners (chicken, duck eat seeds/bugs). Pests (aphid, caterpillar, slug).
Secondary consumersbug eaters
Ladybug, lacewing, parasitic wasp, ground beetle, spider, hoverfly larvae, dragonfly, guinea fowl, barn swallow, duck, chicken. Control pest populations without intervention.
Tertiary consumerstop predators
Hawk, coyote, cougar, bear, human. Affect farm when unbalanced; become neighbors when boundaries work.
Guardiansplayer-deployed buffers
LGD dogs, llamas, donkeys, guardian geese, barn cats. Flip the threat from top predators into neutral pass-through.
Soil food webthe hidden driver
> 1 billion organisms per teaspoon of healthy soil. Bacteria feed protozoa; nematodes poop ammonium; fungi exchange with roots. Everything above depends on this.

The closed loopsEverything feeds something

Chicken eats kitchen scraps → lays egg + drops manure → compost pile heats 15 % faster → compost applied to orchard → apple ripens bigger → cider pressings + apple scraps back to chicken.
Comfrey grows on a tile → chopped by fairy → drops as K-rich mulch at drip line → tree K-up → bigger fruit → fruit feeds fairies → more fairies do more chopping.
Culled animal bones → roasted in kiln → vinegar soak → WCP (water-soluble P) → drenched at flowering tree → bigger crop → more fruit scraps → back to chicken.
Urine diluted 10:1 → applied to cover crop patch → explosive N-loving growth → chop-and-drop → feeds soil + next season's vegetables.
Wood from felled tree → half to building, half to biochar kiln → char + compost = charged biochar → tile CEC bumps permanently.
Roof runoff → rain barrel → reed bed filter → irrigation + reed biomass to compost → soil.
Salmonberry in hedge → flowers feed mason bees → bees pollinate apple (10× orchard yield vs. no bees) → apples ripen in summer → culls to pig → pig manures future salmonberry ground.

The player's progress is something they see, not something they read in a menu.

Self-balancing wild ecology Provide habitat. The ecology runs itself.

The player does not command predators. They do not assign snakes to mice. They build habitat — nest boxes, rock piles, brush piles, ponds, hedgerows — and creatures arrive when conditions are met. A mouse boom triggers an owl arrival when you've built the box. A slug boom triggers duck interest or a garter snake moving into the rock pile. The web self-regulates from there.

The loopHow arrivals happen

Player builds habitat → conditions reach arrival threshold for a species → arrival event fires (banner · camera · audio · bestiary) → species affects populations (predation, pollination, decomposition) → the farm self-regulates without direct command.

The self-balancing cyclePredator-prey in code

Every mobile species has population arithmetic running on the day-tick: population, birth_rate, death_rate, food_consumption. Predators eat prey → prey decreases → predators starve or leave → prey rebounds. The cycle amplitude dampens as biodiversity rises, because more species = more dampening feedbacks.

Outbreaks are the nudgeWhen something goes off-balance

The game doesn't hand you a quest. It shows you a problem, and hints at the ecological answer. You decide whether to build the habitat.

Aphid outbreak in your kale mono-row → bushes wilt visibly → tooltip appears: "Could an insectary strip help?" → you plant one → ladybugs + lacewings arrive within a week → aphids crash.
Mouse population boom in the pantry-rich farm → seedlings chewed at dawn → tooltip: "An owl box attracts a barn owl; a rock pile attracts snakes." → you build → mice stabilize over 2 weeks.
Slug explosion in the wet spring → lettuce shredded → tooltip: "Ducks, garter snakes, ground beetles, and frogs all eat slugs. Which habitat suits your farm?" → you pick one → problem solves over a month.
Grasshopper plague in summer → dry pasture stripping → "Kestrels hunt grasshoppers if they have an open perch. Guineas eat them too." → you place a perch + guinea → flock balances.

Wildlife as ecosystem engineersSome species reshape the land

Beaver

If allowed to remain on-stream, a beaver pair builds its own dam, floods a valley, creates a whole new wetland niche — cattails, salamanders, ducks, dragonflies — that the player didn't plan but now owns. The game lets you decide: trap/relocate, or coexist. Coexistence is more productive in the long run.

Black bear

A bear trail through your orchard is a problem for fruit but it also spreads seeds across the land — salmonberry, salal, thimbleberry establish in places you never planted. Pick your level of fencing.

Deer browse line

Visible at ~1.8 m across unfenced tiles — the deer have a say in what stays short. Use it as a test: fence before planting sensitive orchard.

Salmon run (climax)

A restored salmon stream deposits marine-derived nitrogen in the riparian zone. Trees within 20 m of the stream grow 20–40 % faster the year after a run. Bears + eagles pull fish up into the forest, fertilizing far beyond the water.

The bestiaryEvery arrival is logged

Every species that ever visits the farm gets a bestiary card: species name, first arrival date, current population, habitat requirement, diet, ecological role, and paired species (predators / prey / companions). The bestiary is the player's growing knowledge of their farm's unique ecology.

The magic isn't that you assigned an owl to hunt mice. The magic is that the owl chose you because you made a good home.
